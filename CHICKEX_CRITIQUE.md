# ChickeX Build Plan — Adversarial Review

The plan is unusually strong on framing and self-aware about the classic P2E failure modes. The problems are not in what it *names* — it names everything — but in places where the stated principle and the strawman numbers **contradict each other**, and in scope the doc waves at as "plumbing" that is actually multi-month work. Prioritized fixes below.

## P0 — must fix before writing a line of code

**P0-1. The starter grant is an uncapped, free faucet — it breaks your own invariant #1.**
§4.5 gives every new account **500 CLK + 2 hens + 3 days feed**, and §4.3 commitment #1 says "coins enter only via capped/decaying faucets, NPC floor purchases, or 1:1 deposits." The starter grant is none of those — it is mint-per-account-creation, and account creation is free. 1,000 Sybil accounts = **~810,000 CLK + 2,000 free hens** minted from nothing (my calc). This is *exactly* the SLP-style "play more = mint more," just keyed on signups instead of grinding. **Fix:** make the starter grant a non-cashable, non-tradeable "tutorial" wallet (separate ledger sub-account) that can buy feed and the first NPC interactions but cannot be sold to other players or withdrawn, and that does not count toward circulating CLK. Gate any tradeable balance behind a real action (first deposit, or phone+device-unique verification). Add a hard invariant: `tradeable_CLK_minted == deposits + capped_faucet_emissions` — assert it, alarm on drift.

**P0-2. The headline hen economics are ~2.7× too optimistic; recompute the strawman.**
§4.5 claims "daily net at floor = 3 − 2 = +1 CLK/day." That ignores **yield decline after day 40** and **vet cost** — both of which the same doc mandates. Real lifetime net at floor is **+22 CLK over 60 days (~+0.37/day)**, not +60. Feed+vet is **85% of lifetime revenue**, not the "50–66%" the table claims. This matters because the whole "thin-on-purpose, neglect-loses-money, skill-profits" thesis rests on these numbers, and the *worked revenue example* in §5.3 is built on top of them. **Fix:** rebuild the §4.5 table from a lifetime model (decline curve + expected vet events explicit), publish the real per-day and per-life net, and re-derive the feed-to-revenue ratio so it actually lands in the band you claim. Right now invariant #2 (`feed+vet ≥ 40%`) passes trivially at 85% — the binding constraint is the *upper* bound (does a diligent player still profit enough to stay?), which the doc never states.

**P0-3. Breeding is a faucet, not a sink — it mints both an asset and (via NPC floor) new coins.**
§4.4 lists breeding under "sinks," but a bred hen sold-as-livestock or milked-to-NPC creates **+28.5 CLK of new NPC-floor minting** over its life; the first breed fee (40) barely offsets it and later breeds (escalating) are gated, so early breeding is **roughly coin-neutral-to-positive, not a sink.** The "sink" is only the fee, and the fee is dwarfed by downstream NPC minting from the offspring's eggs. **Fix:** either (a) make NPC-floor egg sales the *only* mint path and cap total NPC-floor purchases per good per day (a real faucet cap), or (b) raise the base breed fee above the offspring's expected lifetime NPC-floor mint so breeding is genuinely net-deflationary. Model this explicitly; don't assert it.

**P0-4. The "NPC floor as faucet" is the uncapped faucet you claim to have eliminated.**
§4.1 says raw yield isn't a faucet because "coins enter only when an asset is sold to the NPC at the floor." But there is **no cap on how many eggs the NPC will buy.** Every hen alive mints `~3 CLK/egg` into existence on demand. With enough hens (Sybil or legit), NPC-floor buying *is* the uncapped mint. The Hay Day comparison is wrong: Hay Day's NPC sinks dominate because Hay Day has no cash-out and no breeding-driven herd growth racing the sinks. **Fix:** the NPC floor must have a **per-good daily purchase budget** (or a price that degrades as daily NPC-bought volume rises — a declining marginal floor). Without that, the floor is a money printer with a herd-sized throttle that players control, not you.

**P0-5. "No cash-out in P1/P2 ⇒ not financial services" does not hold once trade fees are real money.**
The legal spine (§7) leans on "P1 coins non-cashable." But in **P2 you sell coins for real ZainCash/FIB money AND run a 5% house fee on a player-to-player market in those coins.** A regulator/processor does not need cash-*out* to call that a profit-making exchange — users are paying real money for instruments whose price moves and which the house taxes on every trade. The ZainCash "profit-making platforms" clause (§7.1) plausibly bites in **P2**, not P3. The doc treats P2 as safe and P3 as the danger zone; the danger starts in P2. **Fix:** move the "written processor sign-off on the *exact* P2 mechanics (real-money coin purchase + secondary market + house fee)" to a hard gate *before any P2 code*, and get the lawyer opinion (currently scheduled "before P2") to specifically address whether a priced secondary market with a house fee is caught even without cash-out. Do not assume the marketing word-swap (§7.3) survives a processor reading the actual mechanics.

## P1 — fix before P2 / before spending money on it

**P1-6. "Solo + AI" badly under-scopes the AML/KYC/reserve stack.** §6.5 / §7 list KYC, liveness, CDD risk-tiering, transaction monitoring, SAR-style escalation, sanctions/PEP screening, segregated funds, published reserve ratio — described as "plumbing built during P1–P2." That is not plumbing; that is a **compliance program plus a vendor (KYC/liveness provider, sanctions-list feed) plus ongoing human review (SARs require a human).** A solo founder cannot "build" SAR escalation as a code module — it's an operational obligation with legal liability. **Fix:** reframe P3 as **requiring a partner or a licensed EPSP rail that performs KYC/AML for you**, budget for a paid KYC vendor, and explicitly note SAR/PEP/sanctions screening needs an outside provider + a named human reviewer. Treat this as "needs a team/vendor," not "one adapter file."

**P1-7. FIB has no documented payout API — and the doc admits this gates all of P3, yet P3 is in the roadmap as if solvable.** §6.7 #1 and §13 both flag it, which is good, but the whole cash-OUT design (§4.5, §6.3, §10-P3) is written as though local-rail withdrawal works. If FIB can't disburse and ZainCash disbursement is restricted, **cash-out collapses onto the USDT/Kurdcoin bridge** — which re-introduces every crypto/AML/sanctions concern the "local rails first" strategy was meant to defer, plus Treasury-scrutiny risk the doc itself raises in §6.6. **Fix:** resolve the payout-API VERIFY *before* committing to the phrase "local rails first, crypto later" — if the only working cash-out is crypto, your sequencing premise is inverted and P3 should be planned as a crypto-first off-ramp with the legal review that implies.

**P1-8. The market can't have liquidity at MVP scale, so the NPC band *is* the market — and that's a thin-book manipulation surface.** §4.2 is solid in theory, but at P1 (founding cohort, one animal) there will be almost no two-sided player flow; nearly all volume hits the NPC floor/ceiling. The "live price chart + movers + ticker" (§2.4) will be **rendering NPC quotes and a handful of trades** — which is fine, but the doc sells it as real player discovery. Worse, the §8.4 plan to *start* with an AMM-style always-quotes mechanism reintroduces the constant-product value leak the doc explicitly condemns for bonding curves (§4.2) — an AMM *is* a bonding curve. **Fix:** for MVP, drop the AMM phrasing entirely; ship the NPC band as deterministic EMA-trailed quotes (no constant-product math) plus a simple price-time-priority order list. State plainly that early "price discovery" is mostly the NPC band, and don't market a depth chart you can't populate.

**P1-9. Wash-trade defense is under-specified and the trade fee doesn't actually deter the attack you care about.** §4.2 says "the per-fill trade fee already taxes wash trading." But the dangerous wash trade in P2+ is **"A deposits real money → sham trades move value to B → B cash-outs"** (the money-laundering / collusion pattern §7.3 names). A 5% fee is a *cost of laundering*, not a *barrier* — launderers happily pay 5%. Device/IP/funding-source clustering is named but defeated by cheap VPNs/SIMs, which are trivial in-region. **Fix:** the real control is the **same-name-wallet cash-out rule + velocity/holding limits + graph analysis on trade counterparties**, not the fee. Say so, and don't let the fee carry weight it can't bear in the threat model.

**P1-10. Reserve solvency invariant is necessary but not sufficient — it ignores asset-price risk.** Invariant #3 (`cashout ≤ deposits + fees − margin`) protects the *coin* float. But users hold **hens and eggs**, whose CLK value the NPC ceiling implicitly backstops. If everyone dumps inventory to the NPC floor at once to cash out, NPC-floor purchases mint coins that then demand reserve backing — a run can come through the *asset* side, not just the coin side. **Fix:** the solvency model must include outstanding tradeable-asset value at NPC-floor (the maximum coins the herd can mint on a dump), not just circulating CLK. Cap NPC-floor purchase budget (ties to P0-4) so a coordinated dump can't exceed reserve.

## P2 — tighten before launch, not blocking

**P2-11. Floats banned but rounding policy undefined.** §8.1/§8.5 correctly ban floats, but a 5% fee split 3%/2% on integer CLK, plus a 65% NPC floor on a 7-day *median*, generates fractional CLK constantly. Undefined rounding = either a leak or a faucet (rounding always-up mints; always-down burns asymmetrically). **Fix:** specify integer minor-units (e.g. millicluck), banker's rounding or always-round-fee-to-house, and assert ledger-sums-to-zero *after* rounding.

**P2-12. Spoilage timer + offline players = involuntary loss = support/chargeback risk.** §4.5 eggs rot in 4 days, hens die if vet ignored. For a player who travels or loses connectivity (common on flaky Iraqi mobile, which the doc repeatedly cites), this is **real-money-adjacent asset destruction while they were away** — a churn and dispute driver once P2 money is in. **Fix:** add an offline-grace / vacation-pause mechanic, and make sure destruction-while-offline can never touch deposit-backed value (only tutorial/earned coins), reinforcing the two-ledger split.

**P2-13. "Insurance premiums" (§4.1 sink #7) is a legal landmine flag in your own framing.** Selling "insurance" that pays out on a randomized vet/death event is **a contract with a probabilistic payout** — exactly the maysir/gharar surface §7.6 says to avoid, and the word "insurance" itself invites a regulated-activity reading. **Fix:** rename and redesign as a deterministic "vet care subscription" (pre-paid treatment, no probabilistic payout) or cut it from MVP. Don't ship a sink that hands the gambling-reclassifier a free win.

**P2-14. The revenue worked example (§5.3) double-counts engagement and is built on the broken §4.5 numbers.** Trade-fee math assumes "$0.15–0.25 traded/day per active × 5%" with no basis, and IAP assumes ~3% payers × $30 ARPPU in a market the doc *itself* says runs "low per-user." Combined with P0-2 (the underlying coin economics are wrong), these figures are decorative. **Fix:** mark §5.3 explicitly as "unvalidated illustration, do not plan spend against," or rebuild it from the corrected economy sim outputs (§4.5) once those exist.

**P2-15. Missing section: dispute/refund/chargeback *operational* flow.** §7.3 names chargeback risk and "non-refundable virtual goods" ToS, but there's no operational handling — what happens to a player's *bred hens and open orders* when their deposit is charged back after they've already traded the coins to others? This can cascade through the ledger. **Fix:** add a clawback/quarantine design: deposits have a hold/maturity window before the resulting CLK is tradeable or withdrawable, so a chargeback can be unwound without confiscating a third party's assets.

**P2-16. Missing section: data residency / privacy for KYC PII.** You'll be storing national IDs, selfies, phone numbers under CBI AML expectations. There's no mention of where that PII lives, encryption-at-rest, retention, or breach handling — itself a regulated area. **Fix:** add a one-paragraph PII-handling control (encrypted store, access log, retention policy, breach plan) to §9; it's table stakes for the AML posture you're claiming.

## Things the plan gets right (keep)

Two-ledger separation (§4.0), no-bonding-curve / no-leverage stance (§4.2), the no-RNG-payout legal spine (§7.2), double-entry append-only ledger + server-authority + integer money (§8.5/§8.2), the pre-launch economy sim as a hard gate (§4.5), and the VERIFY-before-build discipline on payments (§6.7) are all correct and well above the bar for this genre. The fixes above are mostly "your numbers don't match your principles yet" and "two sinks are secretly faucets," not architectural rewrites.

**Single most important change:** P0-1 + P0-4 together — cap the NPC-floor mint and quarantine the starter grant. Until those land, the economy has two uncapped faucets the doc believes it has already closed, and the 90-day sim will give a false green light because the strawman params (P0-2) understate the leak.
